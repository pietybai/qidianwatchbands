Types of watch straps: six constructions and what each one is for

There are six watch strap constructions worth stocking: moulded silicone, FKM rubber, woven nylon, leather, ceramic links and stainless steel. They differ in water resistance, formality and tooling cost, and almost all run between 16 mm and 24 mm at the lugs. Which one you buy depends on where the watch is worn, not on brand.

Silicone: the default for sweat and water

Moulded silicone is the cheapest way to get a band that survives a gym, a shower and a summer. It is soft, takes pigment well, and a moulded connector can be shaped to a specific case, which is why smartwatch and tracker bands use it. The limitation is formality: silicone reads as sport, not as dress.

Tooling matters more than price here. A moulded band with a cut-out for a specific case needs its own tool, while a flat band with spring bars can be cut from an existing width. Sampling runs 7–10 working days and bulk 15–25 working days after approval.

FKM rubber: silicone with better oil resistance

Fluoroelastomer (FKM) costs more per kilogram than silicone and behaves better around oils, sunscreen and solvents. For a watch worn on a boat or in a workshop it is the more durable choice. Visually it is matte and denser, so it suits a heavy case better than a slim dress watch.

Nylon: the NATO argument

Woven nylon is light, breathes and dries fast. A single-pass NATO keeps the watch attached even if one spring bar fails, which is why field and dive users accept the extra bulk. Nylon typically runs 1.2–1.5 mm thick, so it adds noticeable height under a slim case, and a seatbelt weave looks more formal than a plain weave.

Leather: formality with a maintenance cost

Leather is the only construction on this list that degrades from sweat. It photographs well and matches a dress case, but it needs rotation and care, and it should not be sold to someone who swims in their watch. If a buyer wants one band for everything, leather is the wrong answer.

Ceramic: scratch resistance without a metal look

Ceramic links are fired, polished and about as scratch-resistant as a watch case gets. They are heavier than they look and cannot be resized by bending, so each link is a moulded part. All-ceramic and ceramic-with-silicone are two different products: the first is links end to end, the second is ceramic links on a silicone core.

Stainless steel: link bracelets and full-wrap surrounds

Steel is the heaviest option and the one that reads most like jewellery. Two forms matter: a link bracelet that attaches on spring bars, and a full-wrap surround that holds the case itself. The surround is cut for one case shape, so it is not interchangeable between models.

Zinc alloy: cast shapes with inset stones

Zinc alloy is cast rather than machined, which makes decorative shapes and stone settings affordable. It is plated, so finish quality depends on the plating line, and it is the construction most sensitive to wear at the clasp.

Questions buyers ask

What are the main types of watch straps?

Six constructions cover most of the market: moulded silicone, FKM rubber, woven nylon, leather, ceramic links and stainless steel. Zinc alloy is a seventh used mainly for decorative cast shapes. They differ in water resistance, formality and tooling cost.

Which watch strap material lasts the longest?

Stainless steel and ceramic last longest because neither absorbs water or sweat. Silicone and FKM come next. Leather has the shortest service life because perspiration and water break down the fibres, and it cannot be restored once cracked.

Is a NATO strap better than a normal strap?

A single-pass NATO keeps the watch on if one spring bar fails, which matters for diving and field use. It also adds thickness and raises the watch. For dress wear a two-piece band sits flatter and looks cleaner.

What width do watch straps come in?

Most bands are sold in 16, 18, 20, 22 and 24 mm. Qidian makes 16–24 mm, with 18, 20, 22 and 24 mm held as stock sizes. The width must match the lug gap exactly; a 22 mm band will not compress into a 21 mm gap.

Can one band fit several watch models?

Only if the lug width and the attachment match. A flat band with spring bars fits any case with the same lug gap, while a moulded connector or a full-wrap surround is cut for one case shape and does not transfer between models.
